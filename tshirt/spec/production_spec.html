1Purpose, scope and authority

This document sets out how the official Personnel Department crew-neck T-shirt must be produced: garment, artwork, crest placement, dimensions, print process, quality control, insignia etiquette and disposal of rejects. It applies to every vendor, printer and person who handles the artwork or produces the garment.

  • Mandatory. Every requirement written as "must" or "never" is compulsory. Any deviation needs prior written approval from the approving authority named on the cover.
  • Order of precedence. If documents conflict, the order is: (1) the signed approved pre-production sample, (2) this specification, (3) the master artwork files, (4) the purchase order.
  • Authorized use only. The crest is an official insignia. It is authorized for this order and this quantity only, under the approval reference on the cover.
  • Values marked † are recommended defaults set for this specification. The approving authority may change them before signing.

2Definitions and measuring method

TermMeaning
HPS (high point shoulder)The point where the shoulder seam meets the collar rib, on the wearer's left side.
CF (centre front)The vertical centre line of the garment front, found by folding the garment in half lengthwise, sleeve to sleeve.
Front collar seamThe stitch line where the collar rib joins the front body at CF.
Wearer's leftThe left side of the person wearing the shirt. On a garment laid face-up, this is the right side as you look at it.
Print widthThe horizontal distance between the outermost printed points of the artwork (the ends of the Khmer text line).
PPSPre-production sample: a finished garment made with the bulk materials and process, submitted for written approval before bulk production.

How to measure: lay the garment flat, face-up, smooth and unstretched, on a hard surface. Measure with a steel rule or a calibrated tape, in centimetres. Find CF by folding, never by eye.

8Insignia and design etiquette

The crest represents the institution and must be treated with respect at every stage, from file handling to finished garment. Everyone involved in production must follow these rules.

  1. Reproduce exactly. Use the master files only, at 100% of their supplied proportions.
  2. Never alter the crest. Do not re-colour, stretch, squash, rotate, mirror, crop or partially hide it. Do not add or remove elements, and do not add effects such as shadow, glow, bevel or distressing.
  3. Upright, always. The crown and radiant star must point straight up towards the collar.
  4. Position of honour. The crest may only be printed on the upper torso, at the approved positions: full front centred, or left chest over the heart. Never below the waist, on sleeves, at the hem, or anywhere it could be sat on or folded under.
  5. Clear space. Keep a clear zone of at least 10% of the print width on every side (2.8 cm at 28 cm, 1.0 cm at 10 cm). No other graphics, text, seams or pockets may enter it.
  6. Precedence. No other logo, slogan or graphic may be printed larger than the crest, above it, or touching it. No commercial, vendor or sponsor marks on the outside of the garment.
  7. Approved background only. Print only on the approved military green. Any other garment colour needs new written approval and a new PPS.
  8. Exact wording. Never re-type or re-set the Khmer text in another font. It is part of the approved artwork.
  9. Respectful handling. Printed garments and transfer films must not be placed on the floor, stepped on or used as wipes. Store them flat and covered.

10DTF print and press process

10.1 Printing (RIP)

  • Import the master PNG and set the print width from Section 7. Lock the aspect ratio, and do not resample beyond the master file.
  • Colour: sRGB input, perceptual or relative colorimetric rendering. Match the approved PPS, not the screen.
  • White underbase: 100% under all printed areas, choked by 1–2 px (0.1–0.2 mm) †, with no white outside the artwork.
  • Print a 10 cm test strip at the start of every session and compare it with the approved PPS.

10.2 Powder and curing

  • Apply the hot-melt powder evenly, remove all excess, and cure at the film and powder supplier's specified temperature and time. Record the supplier, product and batch in the production log.

10.3 Heat press

StepTemperatureTimePressure / notes
Pre-press garment (removes moisture and wrinkles)160 °C3–5 s †Light
Transfer press (cotton)160 °C15 sMedium-firm; peel hot or cold according to the film supplier's instructions
Finishing press160 °C10 sMedium-firm, with a PTFE (Teflon) sheet or parchment over the print

Align with a T-square or laser guide using the measurements in Sections 6 and 7. Never align by eye.

10.4 Care instructions (on the care label)

Wash inside out at a maximum of 30 °C · No bleach · Do not tumble dry on high heat · Do not iron directly on the print · Do not dry-clean.

11Quality control and acceptance

11.1 Pre-production sample (PPS)

  • The vendor must submit one PPS per position for sizes S, M and 3XL †, made with the bulk garment, film, ink and press settings.
  • Bulk production may only start after the PPS is approved in writing. The approved PPS is then sealed, signed and kept as the reference standard.

11.3 Bulk inspection

Inspect each lot to ISO 2859-1, general inspection level II, using these acceptance quality limits †: Critical 0 · Major 2.5 · Minor 4.0.

ClassDefects
CRITICALAltered, distorted or incomplete crest; misspelled or re-typed Khmer text; crest inverted, mirrored or tilted; crest printed at an unapproved position; wrong garment colour; any foreign mark on the garment. Zero accepted. Garments with a critical defect must be destroyed (Section 13).
MAJORPlacement outside tolerance; visible colour shift from the PPS; white halo or underbase showing; peeling, cracking or bubbles; ghosting or double image; smudges or stains in the print area; garment hole or seam defect.
MINORSlight gloss variation; loose threads away from the print; small packing or labelling faults.

12Packing, labelling and delivery

  • Fold each garment with the print facing out and centred. Put tissue or a PTFE sheet over the print if it is folded before it has fully cooled.
  • Individual poly bag with a size sticker. Pack cartons by size, with the size, quantity and order reference on two sides.
  • Delivery note to include: quantity per size and position, number of rejects destroyed, and transfer films printed and used (Section 13 reconciliation).

13Security, confidentiality and disposal

  • Master files are confidential. Store them only on the production workstation and never share them with third parties or post them online.
  • Produce only the authorized quantity. Overruns, spare transfers and spare films must be handed back to the department or destroyed under supervision.
  • Rejects and misprints must be destroyed by cutting through the crest before disposal. They must never be sold, donated or reused intact.
  • After delivery, delete the master files from all vendor systems and confirm in writing.
  • Reconciliation (recorded on the delivery note): transfers printed = garments delivered + rejects destroyed + spare transfers returned or destroyed.
